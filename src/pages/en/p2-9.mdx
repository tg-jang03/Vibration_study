# 2-9 Putting the measurement setup together: decisions by purpose

When you first switch on an analyzer, it already holds defaults such as "400 lines, Hann, 4 averages". Can that one setup show the 1X amplitude, the gear sidebands and the bearing impacts equally well?

P2-1 to P2-8 looked at what each setting changes. This page ties them into **one order that starts from "what am I looking for"**, and at the end you check in Signal Lab whether your own setup actually catches the components.

<Callout type="idea" title="After reading this page">

1. You can start from the measurement purpose and choose F_max → number of lines → measurement time → window → averaging → display in order.
2. You can calculate the number of lines you need from the spacing between components and the window.
3. You can check that components above F_max are not folding in.
4. You can state the starting-point setup for each purpose and the reason behind it.
5. You know what to check before trusting a measurement result.

</Callout>

---

## 1. Choose the settings in the order of the questions

<Figure {...F.decisionFlow} />

Each setting needs the answer from the step before it. You must fix F_max before the number of lines gives you Δf, and you need Δf before the measurement time is set. So you ask in order.

| Step | What you ask | What it fixes | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| ① Purpose | What am I measuring for? (1X size, sidebands, impacts …) | The list of components to look at | P1-8, P2-1 to P2-8 |
| ② Highest frequency | Which is the highest of those components? | F_max (with some margin above it), f_s = 2.56 F_max | P2-3 |
| ③ Minimum spacing | What is the spacing between the two closest components? | Δf ≤ spacing ÷ 3.5 (Hann) → number of lines = F_max ÷ Δf | P2-4 |
| ④ Measurement time | Is the speed steady for T = 1/Δf? | If it is too long, revisit ② and ③ | P2-4 |
| ⑤ Window | Accurate amplitude? Separate close components? A small component next to a large one? | Flat top / Hann / Blackman-Harris | P2-5 |
| ⑥ Averaging | Is the condition steady? How much does the floor fluctuate? | Method, count, overlap → total measurement time | P2-6 |
| ⑦ Display | A sinusoidal component or noise? A small component? | Peak · RMS · PSD, linear · dB, units | P2-7 |
| ⑧ Check | Can I trust the result? | The checklist in §5 | P2-3, P2-4, P2-6 |

The answers to ① and ② are set by what the machine contains. Which frequencies the blades, gears, rolling-element bearings, belts and motor put their lines at can be sketched beforehand in the <a href={withBase('/en/p1-8/')}>P1-8 map of frequency bands of interest</a>.

## 2. Following the numbers: a gearbox check

Shaft A runs at 3000 rpm (1X 50 Hz) and carries a 15-tooth gear. The gear it meshes with on shaft B has 60 teeth, so shaft B turns at 12.5 Hz. The gear mesh frequency is 15 × 50 = 750 Hz (<a href={withBase('/en/p1-8/')}>P1-8</a>, P2-6). You want to see which shaft modulates the size of the mesh, by looking at the sideband spacing (P2-8).

<Callout type="example" title="Steps ① to ⑦">

1. **Purpose**: the 750 Hz mesh and its sidebands (± 12.5 Hz or ± 50 Hz)
2. **Highest frequency**: look up to twice the mesh, 1500 Hz → F_max 2000 Hz, f_s = 5120 Hz
3. **Minimum spacing**: sidebands 12.5 Hz apart. To separate them with Hann you need 3.5 bins → Δf ≤ 12.5 ÷ 3.5 ≈ 3.6 Hz → lines ≥ 2000 ÷ 3.6 ≈ 560 → <strong>800 lines</strong> (Δf 2.5 Hz, spacing = 5 bins)
4. **Measurement time**: T = 1 ÷ 2.5 = 0.4 s — short, so there is little worry that the speed changes meanwhile
5. **Window**: Hann (a balance between separation and leakage)
6. **Averaging**: power average of 8, 50 % overlap → $T_{tot} = 0.4 \times [1 + 7 \times 0.5] = 1.8$ s
7. **Display**: the sidebands are smaller than the mesh, so use dB as well, and mm/s RMS

</Callout>

<Figure {...F.exampleGear} />

The top of Figure 2 is the analyzer default of 400 lines. F_max is the same, but Δf is 5 Hz, so the sidebands are only 2.5 bins away and stick to the mesh peak. With the 800 lines calculated in step ③ they are 5 bins away and split apart.

<Callout type="warn" title="Do not measure everything with the defaults">

The defaults are only a starting point chosen to be acceptable on any machine. When the purpose is clear — close components, low frequencies, high-frequency impacts — redo F_max and the number of lines with the calculation of steps ② and ③.

</Callout>

## 3. Checking the upper boundary: components beyond F_max

It is normal for components above F_max not to appear on the screen. The problem is when you sample without an AAF (P2-3) and those components **fold into** 0 to F_max.

<Figure {...F.aafMatters} />

As in the bottom of Figure 3, if an impact ringing near 3 kHz makes a cluster of fake bars at 140 to 740 Hz, you end up hunting for components that are not there.

<Callout type="field" title="Checking that an AAF is present">

A vibration analyzer normally samples after an AAF matched to F_max. But if you sample directly with a general-purpose data acquisition device, there may be no filter, or its cutoff may differ from F_max. If a bar looks suspicious, measure again with a different F_max. A real component stays where it was; a folded-in component changes position.

</Callout>

## 4. Starting points by purpose

Going through ① to ⑦ for each purpose gives setups like the ones below. The values are for a 3000 rpm (1X 50 Hz) machine.

<Callout type="term" title="Components below 1X (Subsynchronous)">

These are components at frequencies lower than 1X. Among them, a component near 0.4 to 0.5X can appear when the oil film in a journal bearing is unstable, so it is checked separately (why it sits at this position: <a href={withBase('/en/p1-8/')}>P1-8</a>; causes and how to tell them apart: Part 7). Because it is close to 1X, you need to allow plenty of lines.

</Callout>

| Purpose | F_max | Lines (Δf) | Window | Averaging | Main reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| General condition check | 2000 Hz (1X × 40) | 1600 (1.25 Hz) | Hann | Power, 8 averages, 50 % | Harmonics and mesh on one screen, and the sidebands separate too |
| 1X measurement before balancing | 500 Hz (1X × 10) | 400 (1.25 Hz) | Flat top | 4 (for phase too, a trigger vector average) | Scalloping loss of the 1X amplitude under 0.01 dB (P2-5) |
| Checking the 0.4 to 0.5X component | 200 Hz (1X × 4) | 800 (0.25 Hz) | Hann | Power, 4 averages, 50 % | Fine resolution below 1X. The speed must stay steady during T = 4 s |
| Gear sidebands | 5000 Hz (mesh × 3 or more) | 3200 (1.56 Hz) | Hann, dB | Power, 8 averages, 50 % | The 12.5 Hz sideband spacing spans 8 bins |
| Rolling-element bearing impacts | 5000 Hz | 1600 (3.1 Hz) | Hann, dB | Power, 8 averages, 50 % | So the 3 kHz ringing is included (why impacts ring at high frequencies: P1-8). The repetition frequency is found with envelope analysis (P5-6) |

<Figure {...F.purposeViews} />

<Callout type="warn" title="The values in the table are starting points">

These values are the result of applying ① to ⑦ to a 3000 rpm example machine. Recommended values differ between the literature, equipment and company guidelines, and they change with the machine's speed and structure, so rather than memorizing them, recalculate in the same order (source comparison is I-014).

</Callout>

## 5. What to check before trusting a result

| Check | How | If it is off | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clipping | Are the tops of the time waveform cut flat? | Raise the input range and measure again | P2-3 |
| 1X position | Does the 1X in the spectrum match rpm ÷ 60? | Check the speed reading and the sensor position. It may be a component of another machine | P2-1 |
| Speed stability | Are the peaks spread wide? | Shorten T, or measure when the speed is steady (order tracking is P5-4) | P2-4 |
| Folded-in components | Is there an unexplained bar near F_max? | Measure again with a different F_max and see whether the position changes | P2-3 |
| Averaging convergence | Do the floor and bar heights stay the same when you average more? | Increase the number of averages | P2-6 |
| Notation | Are Peak or RMS, the unit and the dB reference written down? | Write them in the record too | P2-7 |

## 6. Signal Lab: checking with my own setup

The sandbox below holds a machine with 1X, 2X and 3X, a 0.45X component, gear mesh and sidebands, a rolling-bearing-type impact ringing at 3 kHz, and noise mixed in. When you change the setup, the verdict table tells you, for each component, "visible / merged with neighbor / buried in floor / outside F_max / folded in". You can also open the same test bench larger on its own in the <a href={withBase('/en/lab/sbx-01/')}>Signal Lab collection</a>.

<Callout type="try">

1. Look at the verdict table in the initial state (F_max 2000 Hz, 400 lines, Hann — the analyzer defaults). How are the gear sidebands judged?
2. Change the purpose to "Checking the 0.4 to 0.5X component". What is the verdict on 0.45X, and what is the measurement time T?
3. Choose the purpose "Rolling-element bearing impacts", then lower F_max to 1000 Hz and switch the AAF off. What becomes "folded in", and where do the fake bars appear?
4. With the purpose "1X measurement before balancing", change the speed to 2970 rpm and alternate the window between Hann and Flat top while watching the 1X reading.

</Callout>

<SandboxLab client:visible lang="en" />

In 1, the sidebands are only 2.5 bins apart, so they are "merged with neighbor". In 2, Δf is 0.25 Hz and T is 4 s, so 0.45X (22.5 Hz) is "visible". In 3, the ringing of the impact folds to near f_s 2560 Hz − 3000 Hz → 440 Hz and becomes "folded in". In 4, the 1X at 49.5 Hz lies between bins, so Hann reads about 10 % low, and Flat top reads it exactly as 2.83 mm/s RMS (1X 4 mm/s Peak).

## 7. Summary: Part 2 at a glance

| Setting · interpretation | What it fixes | If set wrongly | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| F_max · f_s | The highest frequency you can see | Components disappear or fold in | P2-3 |
| Lines · Δf · T | The spacing you can separate, the measurement time | Components merge / T is so long that peaks spread | P2-4 |
| Window | Leakage, scalloping loss, separation | Wrong amplitude, or small components get buried | P2-5 |
| Averaging · overlap | Fluctuation of the floor, total measurement time | A jagged floor / a changing condition is missed | P2-6 |
| Display | Peak · RMS · PSD · dB · units | Numbers cannot be compared correctly with other measurements | P2-7 |
| Sidebands · beating | Pointing to the cause shaft by the spacing | Pointing to the wrong cause, or mistaking beating for a fault | P2-8 |

Part 2 covered every setting from a vibration signal becoming a string of numbers to that string becoming a spectrum. The spectrum is the result of those settings, so the same machine looks different depending on them. That is why a measurement record keeps the setup (F_max, number of lines, window, averaging, notation) together with the numbers.

## 8. Review questions

<details>
<summary><strong>Q1. On a 1800 rpm (1X 30 Hz) machine you want to read a 0.42X component to the nearest 0.01X. With F_max 100 Hz, how many lines do you need?</strong></summary>

0.01X = 0.3 Hz, so Δf ≤ 0.3 Hz → lines ≥ 100 ÷ 0.3 ≈ 333 → 400 lines (Δf 0.25 Hz). The speed must then stay steady for T = 4 s.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q2. The sideband spacing of the 1200 Hz mesh is 5 Hz. With F_max 5000 Hz, how many lines do you need to separate the sidebands with Hann?</strong></summary>

Δf ≤ 5 ÷ 3.5 ≈ 1.43 Hz → lines ≥ 5000 ÷ 1.43 = 3500 → 6400 lines (Δf 0.78 Hz, T 1.28 s). If you do not need the whole 0 to 5000 Hz, a Zoom FFT (P2-4) that magnifies only the area around the mesh is more efficient.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q3. You measured with F_max 1000 Hz (f_s 2560 Hz) and found an unexplained bar at 850 Hz. When you raise F_max to 2000 Hz, the 850 Hz bar disappears and a bar appears at 1710 Hz. What happened?</strong></summary>

The 1710 Hz component was folding in to 2560 − 1710 = 850 Hz because there was no AAF. Changing F_max changed f_s and so moved the folded position, which exposed the true position (1710 Hz). Check the AAF setting of the measuring equipment.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q4. You record the 1X amplitude before balancing. Which window do you use, and why?</strong></summary>

Flat top. Even when the speed falls between bins, the scalloping loss is under 0.01 dB, so the amplitude is accurate. With Hann it can read up to 1.42 dB (about 15 %) low (P2-5). In exchange the main lobe is wide, so other components near 1X must be well away from it.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q5. All your checks so far were done with the analyzer defaults (F_max 1000 Hz, 400 lines, Hann, 4 averages). What might you have missed?</strong></summary>

Δf is 2.5 Hz, so sidebands or pairs of components closer than about 9 Hz may have looked merged. The impact ringing above 1000 Hz was not visible, and the 1X amplitude may have been read low by the scalloping loss. Redo ② to ⑤ for each purpose.
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Q6. You measured with T = 4 s to look at 0.45X in steps of 0.25 Hz, but both the 1X and the 0.45X peaks spread wide. Why?</strong></summary>

The speed changed during the 4 seconds of measurement, so the components smeared over several bins (smearing, P2-4). Measure again when the speed is stable, or shorten T and relax the requirement of ③ in exchange, or use order tracking (P5-4), which resamples in step with the rotation angle.
</details>
